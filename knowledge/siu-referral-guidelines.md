# SIU Referral Guidelines

Fictional internal policy written for this proof of concept. It is not legal advice and does not describe any real carrier.

## Purpose and scope

These guidelines decide which auto claims go to the Special Investigations Unit (SIU), in what order, and what has to be in the file when they do. They apply to collision, comprehensive, accident benefits and third-party liability claims in Ontario, Alberta, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick. The SIU investigates; it does not decide coverage. Coverage decisions stay with the claims adjuster and their manager.

## When to refer a claim

Refer a claim to the SIU when any of these is true:

- The claim is in the monthly SIU queue produced by the triage model.
- The adjuster's red-flag checklist scores 3 or more indicators from the red-flag indicator guide.
- The adjuster has a specific, documented concern, for example statements that contradict the photos.
- A provider involved in the claim (clinic, body shop, tow operator or representative) is on the SIU watch list.

A referral is never based on the claimant's age, ethnicity, language, religion, neighbourhood or any other protected ground. A gut feeling without a documented fact is not a referral reason.

## What a referral must contain

Every referral includes: the claim number, a two-to-four sentence summary of the loss, the specific indicators observed (quote the note or document where each one appears), the documents already on file (police report number, photos, estimates, treatment plans), and what has already been verified. If the referral came from the triage model, attach the model's reason codes, but list the indicators in your own words as well. The SIU returns referrals that only say "high score".

## Timelines

The SIU acknowledges a referral within 2 business days and completes an initial assessment within 10 business days. Investigating a claim does not pause the regulatory clock: accident benefits must still be paid or explained within the statutory deadlines, and undisputed parts of a claim are paid on time. Payments may only be held back for the specific part of a claim under investigation, with the reason written in the file.

## Fast-track and straight-through processing

Claims with a calibrated fraud probability below 0.03 can be fast-tracked: they skip the manual red-flag review and go straight to payment once coverage is confirmed. Glass, parking-lot and low-value vandalism claims make up most of this group. To keep the fast-track honest, the SIU audits a random 2% of fast-tracked claims every month and reports how much fraud slipped through. If the audit finds a pattern, the threshold is lowered or the pattern is added to the red-flag guide.

## Capacity and prioritisation

The SIU can open about 20 new files a month. The queue is ranked by expected net saving: the fraud probability multiplied by the claimed amount and the recovery rate, minus the cost of an investigation (about $1,200 per file). Claims whose expected saving is negative are not opened even when capacity is left. The SIU lead may override the order, for example to keep a suspected ring together, but must write down why. A claim that is in the queue but not opened that month moves to the adjuster's enhanced review list.

## Escalation and external reporting

Only the SIU manager may share claim information with law enforcement, industry fraud databases or other insurers, and only through the approved channels. Adjusters do not contact police or other carriers about suspected fraud on their own. Suspected rings that span several claims are escalated to the SIU manager within 5 business days of being identified.
