# Red-Flag Indicator Guide

Fictional guide written for this proof of concept, based on publicly described auto-insurance fraud indicators. An indicator is a reason to look closer, never proof. Most claims that show one or two indicators are legitimate.

## Staged collisions

Staged collisions are usually organised by a ring that reuses people, phones and providers. Indicators:

- The insured says the vehicle ahead stopped suddenly for no reason, often at night.
- Three or more occupants, all reporting soft-tissue injuries (neck and back pain) with little vehicle damage.
- The same clinic and the same paralegal or representative appear on claim after claim, often retained within days of the loss.
- No police attendance and no independent witnesses; the collision is reported at a collision reporting centre.
- The policy is only weeks or a few months old.
- Phone numbers or addresses that also appear on other recent claims.

The strongest single indicator is a repeated pairing of the same representative and the same clinic across unrelated losses.

## Injury build-up and phantom passengers

A real collision is inflated by adding injured people or treatment that did not happen. Indicators: additional passengers come forward after the first report, injuries are first reported weeks after the loss, every occupant claims injury, and treatment plans are submitted before an assessment is done.

## Repair build-up

A body shop inflates an estimate, often through a supplementary estimate after teardown. Indicators: the repair estimate is high compared with the vehicle's value or with similar damage, parts are added that are not visible in the first photos, and the same shop's estimates run well above its peers over several months. Look at the shop's pattern, not one estimate: supplements are normal after teardown.

## Owner give-ups and suspicious thefts

The owner arranges for a vehicle to disappear to escape a loan or a vehicle they no longer want. Indicators: theft shortly after the policy started or the vehicle was bought, a high-value vehicle with no recovery, the insured says both keys are in their possession but there is no sign of forced entry, and ownership or purchase documents are slow to arrive.

## Post-loss policy inception

Coverage is bought after the damage happened, then the damage is reported as new. In Alberta this is most common after hailstorms. Indicators: a policy that started days before the reported loss, a long delay between the storm and the report, and an appraiser's note that the dents show weathering inconsistent with a recent storm.

## Towing and storage schemes

A tow operator arrives at the scene without being called, tows the vehicle to a distant storage yard, and lets storage charges accumulate before demanding a release fee. Indicators:

- The tow truck arrived before police or before anyone called for a tow.
- The vehicle was towed tens of kilometres past several repair shops to a storage yard in another city.
- Storage runs for weeks, with daily charges and a release fee that must be paid before the vehicle is moved.
- Towing and storage charges in the thousands of dollars on an otherwise ordinary collision.
- The same tow operator appears on a sudden cluster of claims across several cities.

These schemes can appear quickly in a new area. A tow operator with no history that suddenly appears on dozens of claims is a priority for the SIU.

## Soft fraud and padding

An honest loss is exaggerated: extra damaged items are added after the first report, or an injury claim is stretched. Padding is the most common kind of fraud and the hardest to detect from the data alone. Handle it through normal adjusting: ask for receipts, photos and timelines, and do not refer to the SIU unless there is a specific indicator.
