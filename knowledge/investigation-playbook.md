# SIU Investigation Playbook

Fictional playbook written for this proof of concept. Steps are listed in the order an investigator usually takes them; skip a step only when it cannot add information to this file.

## First 48 hours

1. Read the whole file, not only the model's reason codes: notes, photos, estimates and treatment plans.
2. Confirm coverage facts: policy start date, vehicle on the policy, listed drivers.
3. Check the identifiers: do the phone number, address, vehicle or providers appear on other claims? Note any link and the claims it leads to.
4. Secure evidence that can disappear: photos of the vehicle, the tow and storage invoices, scene photos, and the vehicle itself if a storage yard is involved.
5. Write a short investigation plan: which facts would confirm or rule out the concern.

## Statements

Take recorded statements from the insured, the driver and every occupant who claims injury, separately and as early as possible. Ask open questions about the trip, the seating positions, where the occupants were going and how they know each other. Inconsistencies between occupants about basic facts matter more than vague answers. Statements are taken politely and without accusation; the claimant is told what the statement is for.

## Vehicle and scene inspection

Have an appraiser or engineer inspect the vehicle: damage consistent with the reported speed and direction, prior damage, and parts on the estimate that are not damaged. For staged collisions, compare the damage on both vehicles. For hail claims, ask the appraiser to date the damage. For thefts, check whether the vehicle had a tracking system and whether keys can be copied.

## Medical and treatment review

Compare the injuries with the damage and the timeline. Look at treatment plans across claims from the same clinic: identical plans for different people, treatment billed before an assessment, and the same representative on most files are patterns worth documenting. Request an independent examination only where the policy and the provincial rules allow it.

## Provider and network review

When several claims share people, phones, addresses or providers, map the links before contacting anyone. A ring investigation is coordinated by the SIU lead so that statements and inspections happen in a planned order. Never contact a provider about another claimant's file.

## Towing and storage disputes

When a storage yard is holding a vehicle and charges are accruing:

1. Move the vehicle to a repair facility of the insured's choice as soon as possible to stop the daily charges.
2. Ask the operator for an itemised invoice, the tow authorisation signed at the scene, and the operator's licence or registration where the province requires one.
3. Compare the invoice with provincial towing and storage rules and with normal market rates in the area; do not pay a release fee that has no basis in the invoice.
4. Record how the tow truck came to the scene. A truck that arrived before anyone called is an indicator of a towing scheme.
5. If the same operator appears on several claims, refer the pattern to the SIU lead as a group, not claim by claim.

## Closing a file

Every investigation closes with one of three outcomes, written in the file with the evidence for it: no fraud found, in which case the claim is paid promptly and the claimant is not told anything that implies suspicion; misrepresentation of part of the claim, in which case only that part is adjusted; or material fraud, in which case a denial requires the SIU manager's and legal counsel's approval. A model score alone never supports any of these outcomes.
